# Night Shelf campaign brief

> Closed evaluation fixture. Northline Public Library and all figures below are fictional. Treat this document as the complete source of truth; do not add external facts, partners, endorsements, or results.

## Client and decision

Northline Public Library operates six neighborhood branches. The leadership team is deciding whether to approve a six-week pilot campaign called **Night Shelf** at the Harbor and East Market branches.

The presentation must help the library director, program owner, and communications lead choose the campaign concept, approve the pilot budget, and agree on the measurement plan.

## Audience evidence

A member survey received 312 responses from residents aged 18 to 30:

- 68% said they read less for pleasure because they "do not have time."
- 54% already visit a branch after 6:00 p.m. at least once a month.
- 41% borrowed at least one digital title in the previous 90 days.
- 27% knew that the Harbor branch stays open until 10:00 p.m. on Thursdays.

The survey did not measure whether late opening hours cause more reading. Do not present correlation as causation.

## The tension

The library already has evening foot traffic, but most younger visitors describe the branch as a place to study, print, or use Wi-Fi. The campaign should make leisure reading feel like a realistic part of an existing evening routine, not another task competing for time.

## Proposed idea

**Night Shelf: one quiet hour that belongs to you.**

Every Thursday from 6:00 to 10:00 p.m., the two pilot branches create a consistent reading-hour experience:

- a clearly marked shelf of short books, essays, comics, and audiobooks;
- 60-minute reading cards that help visitors choose by available time;
- a quiet-zone signal at 8:00 p.m.;
- one weekly social post featuring a staff pick and an exact reading-time estimate.

The phrase "one quiet hour that belongs to you" is the approved concept line for the evaluation. Keep it exact when presented as the campaign idea.

## Touchpoints

The pilot uses three touchpoints:

1. **Street and transit posters** near the two branches. The poster leads with the concept line, Thursday hours, and branch name.
2. **In-branch navigation** from the entrance to the Night Shelf display. It uses the same amber accent and moon-shaped mark.
3. **Social posts** published each Tuesday. Each post names one title, its format, and an estimated reading or listening time supplied by staff.

Do not invent publisher partnerships, author appearances, prizes, food service, or paid media.

## Pilot operations

- Pilot duration: 6 weeks.
- Branches: Harbor and East Market.
- Campaign budget: $18,400.
- Production: $7,200.
- Local placement: $6,000.
- Staff overtime and training: $3,600.
- Contingency: $1,600.
- Launch date: October 8, 2026.
- Decision deadline: September 12, 2026.

## Measurement

The pilot will track:

- Thursday visits between 6:00 and 10:00 p.m.;
- Night Shelf item checkouts;
- QR scans from posters;
- voluntary one-question exit responses: "Did you make time to read tonight?"

The pilot target is a 15% increase in Thursday evening visits at the two branches compared with their own six-week pre-pilot average. This is a target, not a forecast or guaranteed outcome.

## Required close

Ask the leadership team to approve:

- the Night Shelf concept;
- the $18,400 pilot budget;
- the two-branch, six-week scope;
- the stated measurement plan.

Do not close with generic optimism. End on the decision and the September 12 deadline.
