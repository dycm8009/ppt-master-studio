# Taste Lab — Style Specification

> Method and design defaults only. No project communication contract, brand identity, page structure, or SVG prototypes.

## I. Style Overview

| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Style Name | Taste Lab |
| Best Fit | Creative proposals, product launches, narrative keynotes, investor stories, brand-led explainers, and redesigns where generic AI composition is a material risk; use cautiously for dense operating, academic, regulated, or literal-preservation work |
| Reusable Intent | Read the room before choosing an aesthetic, then build a deck whose composition, motion posture, density, and imagery feel specific to the audience and subject rather than copied from a default AI template |

## II. Communication Method

- **Preferred Mode**: custom
- **Mode Behavior**: Start from the confirmed communication contract and source shape. Infer one concise design read covering artifact type, audience, tone, reference family, and overriding constraints. Use that read to select a narrative mode rather than forcing one preferred mode across projects.
- **Argument Flow**: Establish what the audience must notice or decide, choose the evidence and explanatory sequence that earns that outcome, then vary page treatment only when a new communication job requires it. A visual surprise supports the argument; it never replaces the argument.
- **Page Message Discipline**: Give each page one governing job and a legible focal path. Use equal structures for true peers and unequal structures when information weight differs. Avoid repeating a familiar card, split, or centered-title composition merely because the previous page used it.
- **Claim Discipline**: Keep facts, assumptions, scenarios, recommendations, and decisions visibly distinct. Never invent logos, partnerships, metrics, testimonials, interface states, or decorative source labels to make a page appear more credible or designed.

## III. Page Role Vocabulary

| Role | Communication Job | Evidence Obligation | Composition Tendency |
|---|---|---|---|
| Design read | Establish the artifact's specific audience, tone, reference family, and constraints | Derive from the confirmed contract, source material, supplied references, and installed identity | Internal planning role; it does not require a visible page |
| Anchor | Land the governing idea, question, or decision | Use the exact approved claim or ask | One dominant focal relationship with minimal competing chrome |
| Evidence | Make one claim credible | Show the decisive source, value, comparison, example, or quotation with its qualifier | Let the evidence carry visual weight; decoration stays subordinate |
| Mechanism | Explain how parts interact or why an outcome occurs | Preserve actors, direction, dependency, and uncertainty | Use the simplest topology that makes the relationship inspectable |
| Contrast | Expose a meaningful difference, trade-off, or before/after state | Keep the comparison basis and scope explicit | Symmetry is valid for true peers; imbalance is valid when one side carries more weight |
| Sequence | Show ordered change, process, or progression | Preserve order, handoffs, decision points, and exceptions | Maintain one readable path; motion is optional and needs a named orientation or state-change job |
| Reset | Lower information load before a new section or major shift | Carry a real transition, proposition, or standalone emphasis | Use negative space or a single visual anchor; never add a filler page |
| Decision | Close on the actual choice, owner, action, or unresolved issue | Trace the ask to the evidence already shown | Make the required action or judgment unmistakable without generic optimism |

## IV. Evidence & Data Expression

- **Argument Trace**: Every visual emphasis traces to the page's governing claim, and every governing claim traces to approved source material or an explicitly marked scenario/assumption.
- **Charts**: Choose the chart from the information model. State the takeaway, comparison basis, period, units, and uncertainty. Do not use decorative progress bars, fake precision, or a fashionable chart family when native editable geometry expresses the data more clearly.
- **Tables**: Use tables when cell topology is the information model. Keep headers, units, scope, and comparison logic explicit; visual treatment follows density and reading mode rather than a blanket minimalist or card-based preference.
- **Sources**: Preserve citations and source-local qualifiers where they change interpretation. Do not add pseudo-editorial labels, fake field notes, or decorative attribution.
- **Native Editability**: Prefer editable text, shapes, charts, and tables when they preserve the intended appearance and meaning. Use prepared imagery when documentary identity, atmosphere, material texture, or deliberately illustrative expression is the communication job.

## V. Visual System Defaults

- **Preferred Visual Style**: custom
- **Visual Style Behavior**: Infer the visual language from the confirmed audience, artifact type, references, brand assets, and quiet constraints. Reject automatic AI-purple gradients, generic dark mesh heroes, repeated equal cards, glass panels without a grouping job, decorative status dots, invented product screenshots, and random style changes. These are diagnostic signals, not universal bans.
- **Composition**: Judge three internal lenses before page planning: composition variance, motion purpose, and information density. Set each from the brief rather than a fixed baseline. Across the roster, repeat a composition family only when it creates continuity, comparison, or a deliberate page-job arc. Equal peer grids, centered pages, sparse pages, and static pages remain valid when they fit.
- **Density**: Resolve density from reading mode, viewing distance, source burden, and audience familiarity. Use whitespace to separate semantic groups, not to strand content or force unsupported page expansion.
- **Decoration**: Use the lightest boundary that communicates grouping or hierarchy. Cards, panels, rules, gradients, grain, and effects need a page job. One recurring motif may create identity; unrelated flourishes do not accumulate into a system.
- **Color Behavior**: Let confirmed Brand/Deck identity win. Otherwise use a restrained role-based palette with clear contrast and stable accent meaning. Do not ban a color family merely because it is common in AI output; reject it only when it is an unreasoned default.
- **Typography Character**: Choose type character from audience and subject. Build hierarchy through scale, weight, leading, alignment, and space before decorative family mixing. Preserve natural phrase wrapping and viewing-distance legibility.

## VI. Image & Icon Direction

- **Preferred Image Rendering**: custom
- **Image Rendering Behavior**: Match the selected visual language while keeping the depicted subject, evidence status, and source boundary honest. Generated imagery may express an invented or stylized world; supplied or web imagery represents externally verifiable subjects.
- **Image Usage**: Use an image when recognition, comparison, atmosphere, materiality, or a scene materially improves the page. A zero-image deck remains valid after the whole-roster carrier review finds no useful image job.
- **Image Treatment**: Keep crop, framing, scrim, overlap, and texture subordinate to the focal subject and legibility. Do not build fake screenshots from decorative rectangles or add labels that imply a real product state, partnership, location, or source.
- **Icon Treatment**: Use the project-prepared icon pool under the existing library and source rules. Keep one coherent generic family when generic icons recur; real brands use real prepared marks. The Style does not restrict Executor's native-shape or SVG authoring capabilities.

## VII. Review Focus

> Apply this section only after the user explicitly activates visual review. It never triggers that stage.

- The deck's visual language can be explained from the confirmed audience, artifact type, source, references, and constraints rather than from a generic trend.
- Adjacent pages repeat a composition family only for continuity, comparison, or a deliberate page-job arc.
- Visual variance does not weaken hierarchy, evidence, legibility, or template identity.
- Cards, panels, effects, gradients, labels, and decorative marks each perform a concrete grouping, emphasis, identity, or orientation job.
- Equal peer layouts, centered compositions, sparse pages, and static pages remain available when the information genuinely calls for them.
- No invented metric, testimonial, logo relationship, product screenshot, field-note label, or decorative attribution is presented as real.
- Image source and treatment remain honest about documentary identity versus generated or illustrative expression.
- The final decision or ending resolves the communication objective instead of closing with generic optimism.
